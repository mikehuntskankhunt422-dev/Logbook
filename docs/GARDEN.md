# The garden (design)

Design for the journal garden game, from a design review on 2026-10-14. The first release (0.6.0, D97) builds a smaller part of it: plants, sizes, species, the 15 tiers, Growth and one perk per tier. Unlike this draft, it stores nothing in the journal: perk choices stay on each device. Garden update 1, "History" (0.6.1, D98), adds the Herbarium, the Almanac and Time-lapse (VI) and Postcards (VII), adapted to that release: they live on garden pages (#/garden/herbarium, #/garden/almanac), postcards start from a 📮 button on each month's sign rather than a peek card, the Almanac's chart is one series of Growth per month (its tiers are in the rarest-plant line, not stacked colours), and the catalogue number (with the tier the device had when the update came) is kept on the device with the other choices. The sub-pages are titled 'Herbarium · Garden · Logbook' and 'Almanac · Garden · Logbook'. Photo centres at Close zoom wait for zoom levels. The rest is for later updates; numbers may change.

## Summary

Every journal entry grows one plant in an endless garden of month beds, with the newest at the top. The entry's own length sets the plant's size (8 stages, from Little to Flowering tree). Its mood sets the species (10 flowers, plus a Wildflower for no mood). The rank your garden had when you wrote it sets the plant's rarity, on a 15-tier ladder from Common to Worldbloom, and a long entry blooms 1 or 2 tiers higher. Rank comes from Growth, which is earned per day you actually write, on a gentle curve: 100 for turning up plus up to 439 for your own words, so at most 539 a day. Copies, repeats, URLs, number lists and filler add nothing. At that pace a casual writer (3 × 100 words a week) reaches Epic in about a month and Crystal in 9 months, a keen writer (1,000 words a day) reaches Worldbloom in about 21 months, and endless rings (★2, ★3…) follow. Every tier unlocks a perk that works in the first release. Bigger features (time-lapse, postcards, weather, pets, star map, biomes, World Tree) arrive in later updates and unlock for tiers you've already reached. No perk ever changes a default outside the garden without being chosen, and no perk ever locks again. Plants are worked out entirely from the entries. One small encrypted 'garden' key, which needs no format version bump (entry schema 1, folder 3, backup 3), remembers earned tiers, choices and the Growth of permanently deleted entries, so all of it follows sync and backups. Nothing wilts and nothing nags. It ships as a complete 0.6.0 (about 6–7 weeks for one person), followed by four optional garden updates.

## Rules

- THREE AXES, shown in the Field Guide as 'Size is this entry. Rarity is your whole garden.' An entry's own plant words (L) set its size stage and its bloom bonus. Its mood sets its species. The Growth your garden had just before it was written sets its base tier. Mood, title, formatting, tags and the entry's date never change its size, its Growth or its rarity.
- GROWTH WORDS, what counts (core garden/words.ts): the title; every block-level node of text blocks (paragraph, heading, list or task item, quote, table cell, code block); captions on photos, galleries, gallery items, video, audio, files, links and embeds; and link and embed titles. What doesn't count: link and embed url fields, URLs in the text (found with richtext.ts's URL_RE and WWW_RE, which become exported), file names, tags, music links, and a heading that is exactly one of the 40 built-in prompts (PROMPTS moves into core). Today's entryPlainText includes link URLs, so entryWordCount counts 'https://example.com/path' as 4 words (checked); the garden doesn't.
- GROWTH WORDS, tokens. Normalise with NFKC, then toLowerCase() (never toLocaleLowerCase). Words are matched by /[\p{L}\p{N}][\p{L}\p{M}\p{N}'’-]*/gu, which is countWords' pattern plus combining marks, so a 6-word Hindi sentence counts 6, not 8. Runs of Han are cut into 2-character words, runs of Hiragana or Katakana into 3-character words, and runs of Thai, Lao, Khmer or Myanmar into 4-character words. (Today countWords counts a 14-character Chinese sentence as 2 words and a Japanese sentence as 1; checked.) Dropped: words over 40 characters; words of 4 or more characters that repeat one character ('aaaa', '1111'); and every token with no letter at all after the first 25 in an entry, so '1 2 3 … 3000' adds at most 25.
- GROWTH WORDS, repeats and variety. Each node is split into sentences at . ! ? … 。 ！ ？ and line breaks, and a sentence of 3 or more words already seen in the same entry adds 0. Code blocks add at most 100 words per entry. Variety cap, with K = words kept, D = distinct words, C = letters in the words kept and Cd = letters in the distinct words: E0 = round(max(min(K, 4×D), min(C, 4×Cd)/6)). So 'lol' ×1000 gives E0 = 4, while ordinary prose under about 3,000 words is never capped. The letter measure keeps Finnish, German and Turkish fair.
- GROWTH WORDS, copies. This replaces the old rule for pasted paragraphs of 20+ words, which word lists and short lines slipped past. Live and trashed entries are taken in (createdAt, id) order. (a) Sentences and lines of 5 or more words are keyed by cyrb53 (a 53-bit hash) of their normalised text, and one already owned by an earlier entry counts as copied. (b) Fingerprints: every run of 8 consecutive kept tokens, crossing node, line and block boundaries, is hashed with cyrb53 and kept when hash % 8 === 0 (about 1 in 8). A fingerprint seen earlier in the same entry or in an earlier entry counts as copied. copiedShare = max(copied sentence words ÷ K, copied fingerprints ÷ fingerprints), or 0 when the entry has none. E = round(E0 × (1 − copiedShare)). So a 3,000-word list pasted into a second entry adds about 0 there, a daily template line counts once, and a quote you've used before counts once. The check is statistical: copies under about 40 words can slip through, and the day curve below makes that harmless.
- MEDIA ALLOWANCE. Voice: each audio or video block adds 40 per started minute of its media's durationMs (taken as 1 minute if unknown), at most 200 per entry. Pictures: each photo block, gallery item and file block adds 10, at most 50 per entry. Each mediaId counts once across the whole journal, in the earliest entry that uses it. A cover photo and background music (musicId or musicUrl) add 0. Plant words L = E + voice + pictures.
- SIZE STAGES by L, with the number of blooms: 1 Little 0–19 (1 small open flower), 2 Sprig 20–59 (1), 3 Stem 60–149 (2), 4 Bloom 150–299 (3), 5 Clump 300–599 (5), 6 Bush 600–1,199 (7), 7 Tall bush 1,200–2,499 (9), 8 Flowering tree 2,500 and up. Height in garden units (1 unit = 1 CSS px at zoom 1) is h = round(36 + 24 × log2(1 + min(L, 3000)/20)): 0 words → 36, 20 → 60, 150 → 110, 300 → 132, 600 → 155, 1,200 → 178, 2,500 → 203, 3,000 or more → 210. A Flowering tree is drawn at 1.3 × h. Width is 0.6 × h for flowers and 0.8 × h for trees. The bloom radius is (6 + 0.06 × h) × (0.85 + 0.3 × p), where p (0 to 1) is progress through the current stage, so a plant visibly fills out between stages. 'Just planted' (a soil mound with a shoot) is only for an entry with L = 0 and no mood.
- GROWTH PER WRITING DAY, the core of the anti-farming design. Entries are grouped by the UTC day they were created, createdAt.slice(0, 10), falling back to entry.date when createdAt isn't an ISO timestamp. With W = the sum of plant words L created that day: dayGrowth = (100 if any entry created that day has E ≥ 20 written words) + G[min(W, 3000)]. G[w] = round(100 × log2(1 + w/150)) is a fixed integer table: G[20] = 18, G[30] = 26, G[100] = 74, G[150] = 100, G[250] = 142, G[600] = 232, G[1000] = 294, G[3000] = 439. So a day gives 126 for 30 words, 174 for 100, 242 for 250 and 394 for 1,000, and never more than 539, whatever is pasted and however many entries are made. Each entry's Growth is its marginal share: the day's entries, in (createdAt, id) order, each add G[min(before + L, 3000)] − G[min(before, 3000)], where before is the plant words of that day's earlier entries. The first entry of the day with E ≥ 20 also adds the 100, and media alone never earns it. Back-dated and future-dated entries count on the day they were actually written. Every step is integer maths.
- TIER THRESHOLDS (total Growth): I Common 0, II Uncommon 300, III Rare 1,000, IV Epic 2,000, V Legendary 3,500, VI Mythic 5,500, VII Radiant 8,500, VIII Prismatic 13,000, IX Crystal 20,000, X Celestial 30,000, XI Aurora 45,000, XII Cosmic 70,000, XIII Eternal 105,000, XIV Primordial 160,000, XV Worldbloom 250,000. Then a ring every further 100,000: ★2 at 350,000, ★n at 250,000 + (n − 1) × 100,000. The table and the G table are fixed by GARDEN_RULES = 1 and a snapshot test. Any change needs a DECISIONS entry, because it re-tiers every plant.
- GARDEN RANK = the highest tier whose threshold is at or below total Growth, counting live entries, trashed entries and retired rows (see PERMANENT DELETION). The garden header shows it as 'V · Legendary · Grove Warden · 1,240 Growth to Mythic · about 2 weeks at your pace'. Pace = Growth from entries created in the last 28 days ÷ 4, per week. When pace is 0 the estimate is simply left out. The Journal page shows only the rank ('V · Legendary garden'), never Growth to the next tier.
- PLANT RARITY. baseTier = tierFor(the Growth of everything earlier in (createdAt, id) order, including retired rows). The entry's own Growth isn't included, so your first plant is Common and an entry's own edits never change its base tier. Bloom bonus: +1 if L ≥ 600, +2 if L ≥ 1,200, shown as 1 or 2 small ribbons tied to the stem in the tier colour, so standout entries are visible inside a bed. The tier shown is min(baseTier + bonus, XV), and anything above XV becomes 1 or 2 gold crowns. A reveal tells you what's coming ('Your next plants will bloom Rare').
- PACING, simulated with these exact rules, each entry written on its own day. Garden rank at 1 week / 1 month / 6 months / 1 year:
- Casual (3 entries a week, 100 words each): II Uncommon / IV Epic / VIII Prismatic / IX Crystal. X comes at 13.2 months, XIII at 3.9 years and XV at 9.2 years.
- Keen (1,000 words every day): IV Epic / VII Radiant / XII Cosmic / XIII Eternal. Its new plants bloom one tier higher through the bloom bonus. XV comes at 20.9 months and ★2 at 2.4 years.
- Other writers: daily 250 words reaches III / VI / X / XII (XV at 2.8 years); one line a day (30 words) II / V / IX / XI; one 150-word entry a week I / II / V / VII.
The keen writer grows about 5.3× as fast as the casual one, where the old linear rules gave about 23×. Pasting 30,000 words a day is capped at 539 a day, only 1.37× the keen writer (XV at 15.2 months). Pasting a 3,000-word list into 84 entries in one afternoon gives 539 Growth, where the old rules gave 252,050.
- SPECIES come from mood, which never affects Growth or rarity: 😄 Sunflower (#ffd23f/#f29e0c, 16–24 petals, wide open and facing up); 🙂 Daisy (#ffb38a/#f2683c, 8–14 petals, white fading to the colour at the tips); 😐 Tulip (#c9c3e6/#8f86b8, a cup of 3 front and 2 back petals); 😔 Bluebell (#9fbbe0/#5b7fb3, 3–5 nodding bells); 😢 Snowdrop, the flower of hope (#eef2ff/#6f8ff0, outline #3f5fc0 in light theme); 😠 Fire lily (#ff6b6b/#c21f3a, 6 pointed, curled-back petals); 😴 Lavender (#d8cdf5/#9c8ad6, 8–12 florets on a spike); 🤩 Bird of paradise (#ff7ae0/#c2189b, 3 crest petals plus a sparkle); 🥰 Rose (#ffa3c2/#e0457b, layered rings of 5, 8 and 13 petals); 🤔 Iris (#6fd6c8/#1f8f8a, 3 falls and 3 standards). No mood gives a Wildflower (5–8 petals) in the cover gradient's two colours, taken from GRADIENTS in model.ts. With a photo cover it uses one of 6 pastels picked by the seed (#f7b2c4, #b8d8f8, #c8e6a0, #ffd9a0, #d7c4f2, #a8e6dc). Petals always keep the species' own colours at every tier; tier materials go only on rims, centres, stems, auras, particles and ornaments. No species looks sad or wilted.
- IDENTITY. The seed is the FNV-1a 32-bit hash of entry.id, fed to mulberry32 and always drawn in the same order: lean (±8°), stem curve, leaf count (1–4), leaf heights, petal count within the species' range, petal length (±12%), bloom rotation, hue shift (±8°), centre style, and whether there's a side bud. The seed doesn't use rev or tier, so editing makes a plant grow rather than change shape. Shimmering: when seed % 64 === 0 and L ≥ 20, the plant gets an iridescent rim. That's about 1 plant in 64, a pure collectible with no effect on tier or perks.
- TRAITS, all worked out from the entry, and none of them affect rarity. Photos: one speckle on a leaf per photo, up to 6. An audio block or background music: a bellflower on a side stem, with 2–3 notes floating up on hover or focus. Video or an embed: a lantern bud that pulses softly. Country flags in the text: up to 3 pennants hung as bunting. Only regional-indicator pairs found in the flag font's list (FONT_FLAGS in flags.ts, which becomes exported) count, and pennants are drawn as React text with flagEmoji(code), never inside an SVG string. A checklist of 2 or more items, all ticked: a ladybird on a leaf. A link block: a curled tendril.
- NEVER PUNISHING. Nothing wilts, ages, gets weeds or dies. Missed days leave no trace: plants are packed, so there are no empty day cells, and months without entries aren't drawn. There are no 'we miss you' messages, no garden notifications, no daily quests, no streak multipliers and no leaderboards. Mood is never a collection target: no species count, no silhouettes of species you haven't grown, no completion title tied to moods. Copies and filler are never policed in words: they quietly add nothing, and no message ever says so. The existing streak feature stays exactly as it is.
- EDITS. A plant always shows its entry as it is now, so every device shows the same plant. Its size and its own bloom bonus follow its words, and its base tier never changes through its own edits. Removing words from one entry lowers total Growth by at most 539 (the whole share of its day), and every threshold from II up is at least 700 above the one before, so later plants move at most one tier, silently. A plant that shrinks does so over 400 ms (instantly with reduced motion). A growing plant is revealed when you leave the entry.
- TRASH. A trashed entry's plant leaves its bed quietly, with no wilting or ghost plot, and its Growth still counts. A 'Seed tray' by the gate says '3 entries in Recently deleted' (the name Settings already uses), with no deadline or countdown. It leaves out entries that expired by design (expiresAt ≤ deletedAt) and links to Settings → Recently deleted, where the 30-day detail already lives. That list shows each entry's plant with 'Restoring brings its plant back'. Restoring replants the identical plant in the same place.
- PERMANENT DELETION. Purging one entry, emptying the trash, or the 30-day sweep (purgeExpiredTrash) first writes a retired row for each entry into the journal's encrypted 'garden' key: id → [createdAt, w = min(L, 3000), q = 1 if E ≥ 20 else 0, purgedDay]. A retired row takes part in its day's Growth just as the entry did, so purges and expiry never lower your rank or move later plants. A live entry with the same id, for example from a restored backup, takes precedence over its row. Rows hold no words, titles or dates other than createdAt. 'Forget growth from deleted entries' in Settings → Garden clears them: rank can drop, but perks don't lock. 'Delete everything' clears them too.
- EXPIRY. An entry with expiresAt carries a still dandelion-clock seed head, and its peek card says 'Set to leave on 12 Nov' in plain text. There's no countdown, drooping or blowing seeds. When it expires it goes to the trash (TRASH rule), and when it's purged it becomes a retired row (PERMANENT DELETION rule).
- DATES. entry.date decides which bed a plant stands in (so the garden follows your life), and createdAt decides its Growth day and its rarity. Back-dated entries are planted in their past bed and count on the day they were written. A future-dated entry gets a small glass cloche over it until its date arrives, as decoration only. Changing an entry's date moves its plant and changes nothing else.
- IMPORTS, RESTORES AND SYNC. Everything about a plant is worked out from entries and retired rows, so a restored backup, a merge or a synced OneDrive or Dropbox folder gives the identical garden. Only the row wrapping depends on screen width. The garden key travels with the journal folder and inside backups, and it merges rather than overwrites (see technical). Changes that didn't come from your own editing never trigger a reveal. If your rank rose while you were away, the next garden visit shows one card, 'Your garden grew while you were away: now Mythic. New: Garden paths I', with no confetti.
- FIRST RUN. The first time a complete garden computation finishes for a journal whose garden key has no welcomedAt (wherever it runs: the Journal page pills, the editor chip or the garden), there are two cases. (1) Every entry was created since Logbook started this session, which means a brand-new journal: seenTier starts at I, so the first reveals play normally. (2) Otherwise (an existing journal, a restore, a newly synced folder): seenTier = bestTier = rank are set silently, and one welcome card is queued for the next garden visit: 'Your garden grew from 412 entries since March 2023: you're X · Celestial. 10 perks are ready.' The count includes only perks that exist in the installed version. Nothing is written while the journal is locked, while it has 0 entries and 0 retired rows, or while a backup restore is running.
- REVEALS are checked when you leave an entry (route change or Done), after its last save has landed and the entries have reloaded. They're never checked on save, because the editor autosaves 700 ms after every change (SAVE_DELAY_MS). (a) A seed-packet reveal plays only when the rank snapshotted as the editor opened is below the new rank and the new rank is above seenTier. It lists every perk the new tier unlocks, then seenTier and bestTier are saved. (b) Otherwise, if rank > seenTier, the 'grew while you were away' card is queued instead. (c) Otherwise, if the entry is newly planted or its own tier rose, a toast says 'Your entry grew into a Legendary Rose' with a See it button, at most once per entry per visit, and stays 15 s. (d) If it shrank, nothing is said. If the app threw confetti in the last 10 s (first entry of the day, a streak), the reveal skips its petals. Gentle mode: when the entry's mood is 😔, 😢 or 😠 there's no confetti or rays, just quiet wording ('Your words are safe here. A Snowdrop grew.'). 'Tell me what grew' switches (a) and (c) off; the rank still updates silently.
- EDITOR PLANT CHIP (on by default, and can be hidden). A 28px live plant, its stage, and words to the next stage ('Bloom · 38 words to Clump') with a progress ring. It recomputes from the unsaved entry 600 ms after typing stops, using within-entry rules plus a read-only look at the copy table. It never shows Growth or words to the next tier; those live only in the garden header. When a new stage has held for 1.5 s, so deleting and retyping doesn't make it flicker, the chip springs 1 → 1.25 → 1 and a polite live region says 'Your plant grew into a Bloom.' At 600 and 1,200 plant words it adds 'Bloom bonus: this entry will bloom one tier rarer.' There's never confetti while writing, and nothing moves with reduced motion.
- PERK UNLOCKS. unlocked(perk) = unlockAll || max(rank, bestTier) ≥ perk tier, where bestTier lives in the journal's garden key, so earned perks follow the journal to every device and through backups and never lock again. Fairness rule: nothing the app already has is ever locked behind a tier (themes, covers, fonts, Easier reading, prompts, mood, flags, clock, music, export, backups, passcode, streaks, heat map, the existing confetti), and neither are the garden's essentials. Default rule: anything that changes a screen outside the garden (celebration style, accents, the header skin, golden ink, Journal card frames) stays off until you choose it through 'Try it' or the Shed. A garden-only addition (visitors, fireflies, lanterns, starfield, aurora curtains) starts on. A garden-only alternative look (sky mode, paths, borders, gate ornament, signs, ground) keeps the current default until chosen. People with reduced motion get every perk in a still version.
- TRANSPARENCY. The Field Guide (#/garden/guide) explains each rule in plain words, with the numbers above in a folded 'Exact rules' section. It also says that deleted entries keep their Growth, and why. 'Why this plant?' on the peek card lists only what counted: '412 plant words', 'Added 187 Growth on 3 Oct', 'Planted when your garden was IV · Epic', 'Bloom bonus +1 for 600+ plant words', '3 photos'. It never lists what was missing or discounted. The garden always says 'plant words', never 'words', so it doesn't seem to contradict the Calendar's word counts.
- SETTINGS → GARDEN. Following the journal (stored in its encrypted garden key): Show the Garden (on), Show my plant while writing (on), Tell me what grew (on), Plant badges on Journal cards (on), Unlock all decorations (off; for anyone who'd rather not earn them, for example people for whom writing is physically hard; plants keep their own rarity), every perk choice, the garden and pet names, and 'Forget growth from deleted entries'. On this device only: Calm garden (off; turns ambient life off even without reduced motion), Sounds and volume, zoom, and scene or list view.
- DECISIONS TAKEN FROM THE REVIEW. Growth is concave per createdAt day rather than linear per entry. The day bonus is per createdAt day and needs 20 written words. Copies are detected by sentence keys and sampled 8-word fingerprints rather than whole 20-word paragraphs. Number-only tokens are capped. Each media item counts once. Base tier excludes the entry itself. Purges keep their Growth as retired rows. State follows the journal, not the device. Reveals are set up on the first computation. Perks never change a default outside the garden. There is no mood collecting. Postcards hide moods by default. Petals keep the mood's colour at every tier. Rows stack from the ground up. Accessible names use 'Tier 4' rather than 'IV'. Visitors are a fixed 4. The 0.6.0 release has a working perk at every tier.

## The garden

- ROUTE AND ENTRY POINTS: #/garden, with ?month=YYYY-MM to jump to a bed and ?entry=<id> to focus and highlight a plant. Sub-views are #/garden/list, #/garden/guide and, later, #/garden/herbarium and #/garden/almanac. The page's h1 is 'Garden' and document.title is 'Garden · Logbook'. The view is loaded with React.lazy, so app start isn't slowed. Ways in: the Garden menu item, 'Show in garden' in each entry's menu, the reveal's 'See it' button, and the one-line rank link in the Journal header ('V · Legendary garden'). Journal card pills are text only.
- LAYOUT, endless downward. First the gate: sign, rank chip, title, Growth bar, pace, the Shed, the Seed tray, and later the landmarks and the World Tree. Then one bed per month, newest at the top like the Timeline. Each bed is a <section> with a wooden stake sign and an h2 ('October 2026 · 12 plants'). Between years there's a 40-unit stone wall with the year carved in it. Months without entries aren't drawn. The space has no limit: beds simply keep going down.
- PACKING, rows from the ground up. Within a bed, the bottom row holds the oldest plants, left to right by createdAt then id, and each new row starts above the last. So newest is on top everywhere, Up always means newer and Down always means older, and adding a plant never moves an existing one relative to its bed's ground. Each plant takes a slot by stage: stages 1–2 36 units, 3 44, 4 52, 5 64, 6 84, 7 100, 8 120. A seeded x offset of ±6 units looks natural but never moves between renders. Bed width is the container width ÷ zoom, minus 24 units of padding each side (12 on screens under 600 px). Rows are 140 units apart, and taller plants grow up into the row above; lower rows are painted last, so nearer rows overlap farther ones. Bed height = 56 header + headroom + rows × 140 + 24, where headroom = max(0, tallest top-row plant − 140). layoutGarden(items, bedWidth) in core is a pure function, and a test checks that adding an entry leaves every other bed unchanged.
- PLANT A SEED: a '+' plot after the newest plant of the current month's bed (or in a new bed for this month) opens a new entry for today.
- ZOOM, three steps. Meadow 0.4: each plant is a 6-unit dot in its tier colour with a 1px ring (#1d1a17 in light, 9.4:1 on the ground; #f4eee6 in dark) on one canvas per bed, and each bed is a single button labelled 'Open October 2026, 23 plants, rarest Tier 9, Crystal'. Bed 1.0 (the default): full plants. Close 1.5: full plants with title stakes. Controls: −/+ buttons with aria-pressed (needed so zoom works without gestures, WCAG 2.5.1), the + and − keys (only while focus is inside the plant grid or the zoom group, so typing in Find or the sign name never zooms), Ctrl/⌘+wheel and two-finger pinch, all snapping to the nearest step. Changing zoom reflows the rows rather than scrolling sideways, so there's never horizontal scroll at 360 px.
- GETTING AROUND: Today, a 'Go to month' select listing only months with entries, and Random memory. On screens 900 px and wider, a minimap: one canvas strip with a column per month, coloured by that month's rarest plant, plus a text label of the visible month. It scrolls to the bed on click or Enter. Ctrl/⌘+Home and Ctrl/⌘+End jump to the newest and oldest plant.
- FILTERS dim rather than hide, so the layout never jumps: tier ('Rare and up'), species, size stage, tag and trait (photos, music, flags). Plants that don't match drop to 25% opacity and arrow keys skip them, and a polite announcement says 'Showing 14 Rare-and-up plants'. A Find field highlights matches using the existing SearchIndex.
- PLANT INTERACTION. Each plant's hit area is its own slot cell in its own row (slot width × 140 units, at least 36 × 140 px at Bed zoom, well above the 24 px WCAG 2.5.8 needs). The SVG that hangs over the row above has pointer-events: none, so tall plants never cover their neighbours' buttons. Hover or focus lifts the plant (scale 1.04 on its HTML wrapper, none with reduced motion) and shows a stake label with the title (or its first 6 words), date and tier pill. Click, tap, Enter or Space opens the peek card: a popover on desktop and a bottom sheet on phones, built on the existing Dialog. Up and Down go to the nearest plant by x in the row above (newer) or below (older), crossing into the neighbouring bed. Home and End go to the first and last plant in the bed, PageUp and PageDown to the newer and older bed, and Ctrl+Home and Ctrl+End to the newest and oldest plant. Enter opens the card. Moves are worked out from the layout data, so focus can go to a bed that isn't drawn yet: it scrolls there, draws it, then focuses the plant.

## Technical plan

- FORMAT IMPACT: no new entry, media or settings fields and no new gradient id. SCHEMA_VERSION 1 (model.ts), FOLDER_VERSION 3 (storage-fs/folder-store.ts) and BACKUP_VERSION 3 (backup.ts) stay as they are (all checked). The only addition is an optional store key, 'garden', which older apps never read. The folder store already keeps keys as files in .logbook (book-draft.json was the precedent, D96), and 0.5.x leaves unknown .logbook files alone. A backup's logbook.json gets an optional 'garden' field, while 0.5.x importers read only settings and vault (backup.ts line 302, checked). So 0.5.x opens, saves and backs up these journals with nothing lost from entries, and the D91/D93 version bump doesn't apply. Recorded as D97 in docs/DECISIONS.md (D96 is the latest, D95 isn't used), and released as 0.6.0.
- GARDEN KEY (journal state). It adds garden: unknown to StoreKeys in storage.ts. The folder file is '.logbook/garden.json'; IndexedDB and memory stores keep it in their keys store. It's a SealedRecord (cipher.sealJson('garden', 'garden', value)) whenever a passcode is on. The value, validated by a zod gardenStateSchema with .passthrough() at every level so fields from a newer version survive an older 0.6 save: { v: 1, bestTier, seenTier, ring, welcomedAt?, catalogue, name, prefs: { show, chip, reveals, badges, unlockAll }, choices: { celebration, accent, skin, ink, frames, sky, path, border, gate, signs, ground, pet, petName, pet2, pet2Name, biome, weather, hemisphere }, at: { [field]: ISO time }, retired: { [entryId]: [createdAt, w, q, purgedDay] }, forgotAt? }. Each retired row is about 60 bytes. Journal gains getGarden() and updateGarden(patch). updateGarden calls store.refresh?.(), reads the key, merges and writes it. Merge rules: retired rows are unioned (dropping ids that are live, and rows purged before forgotAt); bestTier, seenTier, ring and catalogue take the maximum; welcomedAt takes the earliest; name, prefs and choices take each field's latest value by its 'at' time. Merging is commutative and idempotent, so a write lost to two computers saving at once is restored the next time either device writes. The folder store also merges any sync-conflict copies (.logbook/garden*.json other than garden.json) when it reads, and removes them after the next merged write.
- GARDEN KEY, lifecycle. purgeEntry and purgeExpiredTrash call this.retire(entries) before deleting, and stop the purge if that write fails. retire uses a hook the GardenProvider registers (journal.setRetireHook(fn), which returns rows from the GardenCache). Without the hook, for example in the sweep at launch, it falls back to core's retiredRow(entry, durations), which uses only within-entry rules. rewriteAll() (passcode on or off) seals or opens the key along with the entries. Because a 0.5.x computer can turn a passcode on and leave the key in plain text, 0.6 re-seals a plaintext key whenever a vault exists, on unlock. clearContent() (Delete everything) drops the key along with the book data. Backups: export writes 'garden' into logbook.json, sealed when keepEncrypted. A replace restore sets it from the backup, or leaves none if the backup has none (a 0.5 backup gives the first-run welcome). A merge restore merges it. A 0.5.x app making a backup leaves it out, which the release notes mention.
- CORE MODULE packages/core/src/garden/, pure and DOM-free, re-exported from index.ts. words.ts: tokenise(entry) → a numbers-only EntryWords record { K, D, C, Cd, E0, nonLetter, sentenceKeys: Float64Array, sentenceWords: Uint16Array, fingerprints: Float64Array, media: [{id, kind, durationMs?}] }. hash.ts: cyrb53, fnv1a32, mulberry32. owners.ts: OwnerTable, an open-addressing table (Float64Array keys, Int32Array owner order) sized to the next power of two at least 1.7× the keys. growth.ts: computeGarden({ live, trash, retired, durations }) → { plants, growth, rank, ring, next, pace28d, writingDays }, and the G table. tiers.ts: TIERS (id, numeral, name, title, min, light and dark badge colours), GARDEN_RULES = 1, tierFor(), nextTier(), ringFor(). stages.ts: STAGES, stageFor(L), plantHeight(L). plant.ts: plantSpec(entry, L, tier, bonus) → { species, stage, h, w, blooms, palette, layers: { rim, centre, aura, particles, ornament, stem }, traits, ribbons, crowns, shimmering }, and plantParts(spec, lod) → [{ d, fill, opacity }]. layout.ts: layoutGarden(items, bedWidth) (rows from the ground up) and gardenNeighbour(layout, id, key). state.ts: gardenStateSchema, mergeGardenState(), retiredRow(). perks.ts: PERKS (tier, feature id, catalogue number), perksFor(rank, bestTier, unlockAll). PROMPTS moves from apps/web/src/lib/prompts.ts into core, and the web module re-exports it.
- ART SYSTEM: 4 shared builders draw all 11 species. Radial draws Sunflower, Daisy, Fire lily, Rose, Iris and Wildflower; cup/bell draws Tulip, Bluebell and Snowdrop; spike draws Lavender; crest draws Bird of paradise. Each builder takes the stage (bloom count, stem height) and the seed. Tier looks are composed from 5 layers plus a stem style (rim, centre, aura, particles, ornament), each a small set of path or gradient recipes chosen per tier in one TIER_LAYERS table. That replaces 15 hand-made materials, and petals always keep the species colour. A dev-only #/garden-lab route (import.meta.env.DEV) shows every species × stage × tier, and a full bed at each of XI–XV, for tuning. About 20 KB of builders.
- DETERMINISM: no Intl.Segmenter, no toLocaleLowerCase, no local-time maths except reading the entry.date string, and integer Growth maths throughout. G is built with Math.round, and a test checks that no G value lies within 1e-6 of a .5 boundary, so engines that differ by one ulp in Math.log2 still agree (0 such values, checked). Today's date is used only for decoration (sky, moon, seasons, weather, cloche). Ordering uses createdAt ISO strings and ids. The same journal therefore gives the same plants on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and the browser, and in any time zone, for text within Unicode 15. Newer code points can be classified differently by older webview engines, and fixtures pin some recent CJK extension characters. App-wide countWords and the heat map (useDayStats in views.tsx) are left unchanged.
- PIPELINE: a new Journal.listEntriesWithTrash() returns { live, trash } from one readAllEntries(true) pass. That costs nothing extra, since readAllEntries(false) already decrypts and parses every record, trash included, before filtering it out (journal.ts lines 284–296, checked). JournalProvider gains `trash`, while Timeline, Calendar and Search keep using `entries`. Media durations come from journal.listMediaMeta() as a Map<mediaId, durationMs>; media-import.ts stores durationMs for both audio and video (checked). The map is refreshed on the 'media' event, and the garden recomputes on 'entries'. The retired rows come from getGarden(), refreshed on 'entries'. JournalEvent is 'entries' | 'media' | 'settings' | 'lock' (checked).
- CACHING: JournalProvider re-reads every entry on each 'entries' event, which creates new objects (checked), so the cache is keyed by `${id}:${rev}:${updatedAt}` rather than by object. GardenCache keeps the numbers-only EntryWords per entry. On each 'entries' event only changed entries are tokenised. The OwnerTable is then rebuilt from the cached hash arrays in (createdAt, id) order (about 3 ms for 1,000 × 250 words, and 30–60 ms for 10,000 × 554 on desktop), followed by one pass for copy shares, day shares, running sums and tiers. The cache holds hashes and counts, never text. It stays in memory across the auto-lock (default 5 minutes), so unlocking costs no re-tokenising. It is never written to disk (D8), and it's dropped when the app closes or the journal folder changes. Garden output that holds titles (plants, labels) is cleared on 'lock'.
- MEASURED COST: the review prototyped this pipeline (NFKC, lowercase, the word regex, sentence sets, a full-string owner map, the variety cap) in Node on desktop: 5,000 × 554 words took 0.73 s and 10,000 took 1.5 s, using 44–70 MB of heap. Hashed keys bring memory to about 1.5 MB for a 1,000 × 250-word journal and about 25 MB of tables plus 10 MB of per-entry arrays for 10,000 × 554. Phones are 3–5× slower: about 2–4 s for 5,000 entries and 4–7 s for 10,000, once per app launch. The first pass runs in chunks of 200 entries with requestIdleCallback (falling back to setTimeout 0 on WebKit), starting 2 s after the first load when the garden is shown. No task may exceed 50 ms. Until the pass finishes, beds show stage-sized neutral plants under 'Growing your garden…', and Journal card pills appear once it's ready. The release budget is at most 4 s for 5,000 entries on a low-end Android phone. If that's missed, tokenising moves into a module Web Worker, since EntryWords is already plain numbers.
- DEVICE STATE: only per-device UI preferences stay in localStorage: { v: 1, zoom, calm, sounds, volume, view }. On desktop the key is 'logbook.garden:' + fnv1a32(the journal folder path from journalFolder()).toString(36), so 'Open a different journal folder…' (JournalFolderPanel.tsx) never carries state across. The browser and Android have one journal per origin, so they use 'logbook.garden'. It never holds entry ids, titles, names, tiers or counts. Code lives in apps/web/src/lib/garden-prefs.ts with try/catch around every read and write, so private browsing just means defaults.
- RENDERING: DOM plus inline SVG, not a canvas scene. A real <button> per plant gives focus, names and axe coverage for free. Canvas is used only where text and focus don't matter: Meadow dots, the minimap, postcards and the weather overlay. Each plant's SVG string comes from plantParts() and is inserted with dangerouslySetInnerHTML, cached per (id, rev, lod, theme). One shared <svg><defs> holds about 60 gradients with fixed ids (15 tier recipes, 22 species stops, 10 cover gradients, 6 wildflower pastels, glows, sky). It's hidden with width and height 0 and position: absolute, not display: none, which breaks url(#id) fills in Firefox and some WebKit versions. Rays are drawn as paths, because SVG has no conic gradients.
- SVG SAFETY: plant SVG strings contain only numbers, path commands, colour constants from a fixed table, fixed gradient ids, and the digits of entry.date (restricted to \d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2} by ISO_DATE) for the Eternal plinth. Titles (Close-zoom stakes), flag pennants and garden and pet names are React text nodes in an HTML overlay, never part of a string inserted as HTML. This matters because in Tauri a crafted title arriving from a synced folder or a backup would otherwise run with access to the bridge (saveFile and the other invoke commands). A unit test checks every plantParts and plant-SVG string for every species × stage × tier against a whitelist of tags (svg, g, path, circle, ellipse, rect, text), attributes and value characters.

## Risks

- Writing for the plant instead of for yourself (Goodhart's law). The day curve caps any day at 539 Growth and makes extra words worth less and less, so the best strategy is simply to write often. Copies, repeats, number lists, URLs and filler add nothing. The editor chip shows only this entry's size and can be hidden, there are no leaderboards, and the copy says 'Write what you want; the garden follows'. Some people will still chase tiers; the design only promises that chasing never needs filler.
- The brief's 'the more you write, the more rare it will become' could mean each entry's own length rather than total writing. The design covers both readings (a long entry blooms +1 or +2 and grows bigger, and your total raises every new plant), but if the user meant per-entry only, the ladder and perk pacing need rework (open question 1).
- The day bonus favours turning up: one line a day (30 words) reaches XI in a year, while 3 × 100 words a week reaches IX. That suits engagement, but it weighs writing often above writing a lot (open question 2).
- Keyboard mashing and pasted outside text that varies (articles, AI-written prose) can't be detected offline and still grow plants. They can earn no more than an honest heavy day (539), so this is accepted: it only cheats the person themselves, and policing a private journal would harm everyone else.
- Copy detection is statistical (1-in-8 fingerprints and 5+-word sentence keys). Copies under about 40 words can slip through, and honest phrases shared with older entries (a favourite quote, a daily template) lower that entry's credit a little, silently.
- Retired rows mean a permanently deleted entry leaves a creation time and two numbers behind in the journal (encrypted when the passcode is on), which someone may not expect from 'permanent'. The Field Guide explains it and 'Forget growth from deleted entries' clears them (open question 3).
- Mixed app versions: a computer still on 0.5.x that purges entries writes no retired rows, so rank can drop until every computer runs 0.6. A backup made by 0.5.x leaves out the garden key. A 0.5.x passcode change leaves garden.json in plain text until a 0.6 app unlocks the journal and re-seals it.
- Sync: two computers writing garden.json at the same moment can lose one write. Merging restores it on the next write, and conflict copies are merged, but for a short time a choice or a reveal record can differ between devices.
- UTC days: a writer in UTC−8 who writes at 3 pm and again at 5 pm local time crosses UTC midnight and earns two day bonuses. This is harmless and recorded in D97. Changing the system clock or hand-editing a backup's createdAt could fake writing days, and only cheats yourself.
- A veteran's very short entry still shows a high tier, and a beginner's long entry a low one. Size, ribbons and the Field Guide ('Size is this entry. Rarity is your whole garden.') explain it, but it can still feel odd.
- A painful entry can become a dazzling plant. Gentle mode (no confetti or rays and quiet wording for 😔 😢 😠) and the 'Tell me what grew' switch soften this.
- Language rules are approximations (2 characters per Han word, 3 per kana word, 4 per Thai word, letters ÷ 6). They need native-speaker fixtures before release, and any later change re-tiers everyone, hence GARDEN_RULES and a decision entry for any change.
- Performance on low-end Android and WebKitGTK with very large journals: about 2–7 s of idle-time counting at each launch for 5,000–10,000 entries, plus SVG nodes and particles. Mitigations: hashed tables, the numbers-only cache, idle chunks, windowed beds, no filters, sway on composited wrappers, and caps on animated plants. Phase 1 has to measure it on a real phone, with a Worker as the fallback.
- Art scope is the biggest time risk: 11 species × 8 stages × 15 tiers drawn procedurally could look like programmer art. Four shared builders, the 5-layer tier table and the garden-lab route keep it tractable, but it still has to ship complete in phase 1.
- Scope: phase 1 alone is 6–7 weeks for one person, and all phases together 9–11 weeks. The later updates can be cut freely, but phase 1 can't shrink much without losing the promise that every tier gives something.
- Accessibility of a spatial scene: axe won't catch everything. The list view, roving focus and labels must stay as good as the visual garden, and a manual screen-reader pass (NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack) is part of phase 1.

## Open questions

- Rarity model: keep 'rarity is your whole garden' (a plant's tier is your garden's rank when you wrote it, +1 for 600+ plant words and +2 for 1,200+), as designed? Or make rarity depend only on each entry's own length? Length-only is simpler, but every tier could then be reached in a single long sitting, and the perks would all unlock in a day.
- How much should turning up count against writing a lot? As designed, each writing day gives 100 plus up to 439 for words. So one line a day (30 words) reaches Aurora (XI) in a year, while 3 entries a week of 100 words reaches Crystal (IX), and 1,000 words a day reaches Eternal (XIII). Halving the day bonus to 50 would weigh words more: the 3-a-week writer would reach Prismatic (VIII) in a year and the one-liner Crystal (IX).
- Should permanently deleted entries keep their Growth? As designed, the journal keeps an encrypted note of each one's creation time and plant words, so purges and expiring entries never lower your garden, and there's a 'Forget growth from deleted entries' button. The alternative is that deleting removes the Growth too, so your rank and later plants can drop by a tier.
- Should voice and video add to plant words (40 a minute, up to 200 an entry) and pictures too (10 each, up to 50), as designed? Or should only written words count?
- Are 15 tiers with these names right (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic, Radiant, Prismatic, Crystal, Celestial, Aurora, Cosmic, Eternal, Primordial, Worldbloom, then endless ★ rings)? Or would you like garden-themed names, or a different count?
