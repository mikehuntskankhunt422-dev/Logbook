# Milestone 4: Lulu fulfilment

Status: **in progress, 2026-10-08.** Everything up to "Lulu has the book" works against the real services: in the Lulu sandbox and Stripe test mode, paid orders for the 40-page and 200-page samples became accepted Lulu print jobs holding exactly the approved files, and a job Lulu rejected was refunded in full (§3). Emails go out through Resend: a test alert reached Resend and was accepted (§3). The sandbox stops every job at `UNPAID` until a test card is on file; real webhook deliveries and emails to customers wait on things only you can provide (§6) · Expands [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) §1.5 steps 7–10, §1.6, §3 (M4) and §9 risk #1 · Author: Claude

## 0. In one paragraph

M4 sends paid books to Lulu and follows them to the customer's door. After Stripe says "paid", a job confirms the print files are the ones the customer approved, has Lulu check them again, and creates the Lulu print job with the address Stripe collected. Lulu's webhooks (and polling when they're missed) move the order through `in_production`, `shipped` and `delivered`. Anything that goes wrong after payment is retried with backoff, then becomes `needs_attention` with an email to you; a book Lulu can't print is refunded in full automatically. **Done means** the state-machine and fulfilment unit tests pass, and in the Lulu sandbox (with Stripe test mode) real print jobs are created for the 40-page and 200-page samples and followed to the furthest status the sandbox reaches, and a simulated failure ends in a Stripe refund.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 Fulfilment (PLAN §1.5 step 7)

A paid order gets a `fulfil` job, queued **in the same transaction** that marks it paid, so a crash can't lose it. The job advances one state per step; each step is idempotent and safe to repeat:

1. `paid → files_generated`: both print PDFs are still in storage and their SHA-256 equals the hashes recorded when they were rendered (new: recorded at render, migration 4). Missing files or a different hash can't be fixed by retrying: refund.
2. `files_generated → files_validated`: Lulu's validators again, with short signed links (D10). Lulu reuses results for files it has seen, so this is quick. `ERROR`: refund.
3. `files_validated → submitted_to_lulu`: look for a job with `external_id` = order ID; if none, create one with each file's `source_md5sum` (D66), the shipping address, phone and email from Stripe, the paid shipping level, `recipient_tax_id` for BR/CL/MX, `contact_email` = yours, and the title "Logbook journal" rather than the book's (the database has no journal text, PLAN §6).

### 2.2 Tracking (PLAN §1.5 step 8)

- `POST /api/lulu/webhook`: HMAC checked on the raw body. A verified webhook only says "look again": the server reads the job from Lulu's API and applies that, so a replayed or out-of-order webhook can't move an order backwards.
- A `track` job per submitted order reads the job every 6 hours (configurable), and is pushed back whenever a webhook arrives. Tracking stops at `delivered`, `needs_attention` or `refunded`, or 30 days after `shipped` (not every carrier reports delivery).
- Mapping: `IN_PRODUCTION` → `in_production`; `SHIPPED` → `shipped` (tracking links stored, email to the customer); `DELIVERED` → `delivered`; `REJECTED` → `needs_attention` then automatic refund; `CANCELED` and `ERROR` → `needs_attention` and an email to you. Everything else stays `submitted_to_lulu`.

### 2.3 Failures (PLAN §1.5 step 9)

- Jobs live in a SQLite `jobs` table (D13): due time, attempts, last error. One runner, one job at a time. Backoff 1, 2, 4 … minutes capped at 60, at most 8 attempts (about 2 hours), then `needs_attention` and an email to you (and a short note to the customer).
- Can't be fixed by retrying (files gone or changed, Lulu `ERROR` on validation, `REJECTED`): `needs_attention`, then a `refund` job: a full Stripe refund with idempotency key `refund:<order>` → `refunded`, and an email to the customer.
- Lulu refusing the address (400) needs a person: `needs_attention`, no automatic refund.
- Fault injection, test mode only (`LOGBOOK_FAULTS`, refused when `APP_MODE=live`): `lulu-reject` sends a wrong `source_md5sum` so the **real** sandbox rejects the job; `files-missing` and `lulu-down` exercise the other two paths.

### 2.4 Files after payment (D65's open end)

Lulu copies the files when it accepts the job (§1), so paid orders don't need them kept longer. Checkout refuses print files more than 6 days old, so a paid order always has at least a day of retries before the 7-day lifecycle rule deletes them (D71).

### 2.5 Emails

An outbox in the `jobs` table: `email` jobs, one per (order, template), sent through Resend with an idempotency key. Templates carry no journal content: the order number, product, status and tracking link. Without `RESEND_API_KEY` the job records that the email wasn't sent and logs only the template and order. Customer: problem, shipped, refunded. You (`OWNER_EMAIL`): needs attention.

### Emails through Resend (2026-10-08)

`npm run email:check -w @logbook/server` (Node 24.21.0) makes up an order in a temporary database, moves it to `needs_attention` and lets the real job runner run its `alert` email job, so the email goes out as a real alert would: the `alert` template, from `EMAIL_FROM` to `OWNER_EMAIL`, with an idempotency key. It touches nothing else.

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Config: `RESEND_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM` (`Logbook <onboarding@resend.dev>`), `OWNER_EMAIL` | All three set and accepted by `loadConfig` |
| `POST https://api.resend.com/emails` | **Accepted**: email `01a11c32-c337-7c0a-a263-c72f0797aa96`, the email job `done` on its first attempt, subject "Logbook: order ord_emailcheck_e11f8d47 needs attention" |
| `GET /emails/{id}` (did it arrive?) | 401 `restricted_api_key`: the key can only send, which is right for the server, so arrival can only be checked in your inbox or Resend's dashboard |

What this doesn't cover: the sender is Resend's shared test address, `onboarding@resend.dev`, which Resend lets send only to the address the Resend account belongs to. Alerts to you work with it; emails to customers (`problem`, `shipped`, `refunded`) need a domain of yours verified in Resend and `EMAIL_FROM` on it (§6).

`S3_ACCESS_KEY_ID` is no longer set in this environment while the other three `S3_*` variables are, and `loadConfig` refuses that (all four or none), so the check ran with the `S3_*` variables unset; it doesn't use the bucket. `lulu:e2e`, `stripe:check` and the server itself won't start here until the key is back (§6).

## 5. Risks specific to M4

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The sandbox never gets past `UNPAID` without a card on file (now confirmed) | Ask for a test card in the sandbox account (§6); the e2e reports the furthest status honestly. The webhook `test-submission` endpoint and a fake Lulu cover the later states in tests (PLAN risk #1) |
| Webhook signature encoding unknown (#2) | Accept hex or base64 of the correct HMAC, log which; a verified webhook only triggers a fresh read from Lulu's API |
| A Stripe address doesn't fit Lulu's limits (name 35, street and city 30, PLAN risk #7) | Split at word boundaries into `street1`/`street2`; otherwise `needs_attention` and a note to the customer |
| Lulu's sandbox accepts things production refuses | Production isn't reachable from here; first live order is watched by hand (M6 go-live checklist) |
